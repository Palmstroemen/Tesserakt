# How to read your result {#skill}

Kurz: The skill shows whether a horoscope needs your date of birth to describe you – or whether any other date would fit just as well. 0 means chance, +100 means better than all the others.

## What the skill measures {#was_misst_skill}

Every horoscope claims: your date of birth says something about you. That is exactly what we test.

To do this, we calculate each horoscope not only for **your** date of birth, but also for **100 other, randomly chosen birthdays**. Then we look at which of these horoscopes fit your answers best.

- If your own horoscope fits better than all 100 others, the horoscope gets **+100**.
- If it fits only as well as any other one, it gets **0**.
- If it fits worse than all the others, it gets **-100**.

So a skill of **+60** means: your own horoscope fit you better than 80 of 100 strangers' horoscopes of the same tradition.

## Why one horoscope almost always shines {#gewinner_effekt}

Imagine you roll seven dice. Almost always at least one of them shows a five or a six – not because that die is special, but because there are seven of them.

It is much the same with the seven horoscopes. Even if not a single one of them knew anything about you, the best would reach a skill of about **+75** on average. A high score for the winner is therefore perfectly normal and no proof yet.

## For other people, a different horoscope wins {#verteilung_der_siege}

Your winner is your personal result – for your friend Bazi might win, for your coworker the Kabbalah. If no horoscope really knows anything, the wins spread out evenly over many games: each horoscope then wins for roughly one in seven people.

By the way: if you play again yourself, the same horoscope often wins again. That is because your date of birth and your answers stay the same – it says nothing yet about how accurate the horoscope is.

## Where it shows whether a horoscope really hits the mark {#viele_spiele}

A single game cannot answer this question. Many games together can:

- If a horoscope knew nothing about people, its **average** across all games would be **0**, and it would not win more often than the others.
- If a horoscope knew something, its average would be **clearly above 0**, and it would win strikingly often.

That is exactly why your game counts: every round is a small building block in a large experiment.

## And the fit? {#fit}

The **fit** shows how close a horoscope's statements come to your answers – regardless of whether your date of birth was needed for that. It is easy to grasp, but for the question "Does the horoscope really hit the mark?" the **skill** is the value that counts.
