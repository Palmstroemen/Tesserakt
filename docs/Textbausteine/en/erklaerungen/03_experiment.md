# The experiment behind the game {#experiment}

Kurz: Tesserakt is a game and an open experiment at the same time. From many anonymous games we want to find out whether horoscopes describe people better than chance.

## The question {#die_frage}

For thousands of years people have read meaning into the stars, numbers and calendars. We want to know: does a horoscope describe people better when it knows their **real** date of birth than when it is given a random one?

We go into this experiment without a ready-made answer. The result may turn out differently for each horoscope.

## Why a single game is not enough {#ein_spiel_reicht_nicht}

A single result depends heavily on chance – on how you answer today, and on which of the seven horoscopes happens to fit. Only when many people play does this chance average out. Then it shows whether a horoscope does better on average than chance would lead us to expect.

## What we compare {#was_wir_vergleichen}

- **Your self-assessment:** 16 statements that you rate for yourself.
- **The horoscopes:** seven traditions, each calculated for your date of birth.
- **The control group:** the same horoscopes for 100 other, random birthdays. It shows how well a horoscope would fit "by chance".

## What happens to your data {#daten}

- You do not need an account.
- We store your date of birth, the time, your answers and the result – without your name.
- Of your place of birth we store only the time zone and a rough region (about 100 km).
- If you give your name, only numerology uses it, for its name numbers. These three numbers are stored, not the name.
- Only aggregated, anonymous analyses across many games are published.

## How honest is the self-assessment? {#selbsteinschaetzung}

Nobody sees themselves entirely objectively. That is not a flaw but part of the experiment: we compare the horoscopes with the picture people have of themselves. Because all horoscopes are measured against the same answers, the comparison is fair all the same.

## Join in {#mitmachen}

Every round is a small building block. If you share the game, the experiment gains in power – and perhaps you will discover along the way how differently the horoscopes see one and the same person.
